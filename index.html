Case study

Why affordability is so hard to keep, even in one building

The financial model is the easy part. The hard part is getting four parties to say yes to a structure none of them had used before, when each one needs something different.

  1. The ownerTo stay in his home for life, and to know what happens to the building after.
  2. Development partnerA return that justifies a small, slow, unusual deal.
  3. Legal counselA life estate and equity swap that holds up, with few precedents to lean on.
  4. City councilAffordability that lasts, and a model worth backing again.

I built open feasibility tools so every party could look at the same numbers. They compare three paths:

Path A

Keep it as permanent affordable rental, as the owner intends

Path B

Condo sale that plans for succession

Path C

Side-by-side comparison and a 10-step Cambridge action plan for legal and city review

POPL

Why housing gets stuck, and how to unstick it

I founded POPL in 2024 because the housing crisis is only partly about money and land. It's a coordination problem. The capital, the owners, and the communities that could preserve a building rarely meet on the same deal.

Capital

Big funds can't write a check small enough for a triple-decker.

Owners

Long-time owners hold equity they can't use without selling and leaving.

Communities

Local groups know the buildings but can't reach institutional money.

POPL works in the space between them.

  • Consulting. Advising mission-driven organizations and capital partners on housing strategy, structuring, and financing.
  • New ownership models. Structures like the life estate and equity swap, tested on our own pilot first.
  • Research and writing. Crime Pays, Housing Doesn't, my Substack.
